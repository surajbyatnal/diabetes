What this tool does

Glucoscope gives a simple screening result based on eight measurements and a statistical model called logistic regression.

The model is trained on 768 Pima Indian women aged 21 and over. Anyone aged 21–81 can use the tool, but its results have not been validated for men or other populations.

Who it is for

  • Adults aged 21 to 81 who want an educational screening result.
  • Anyone aged 21–81 who understands the model was trained and tested on Pima Indian women only.

Predictions for men and populations not represented in the dataset may be inaccurate. This tool is not for people with a diabetes diagnosis.

What it cannot do

  • It cannot diagnose diabetes. Only a blood test ordered by a doctor can do that.
  • A result that does not flag diabetes cannot rule it out.
  • It reflects one group of people. The results may not apply equally to every population.
  • The percentages describe similarity to the dataset, not your personal chance. About 1 in 3 women in the dataset had diabetes, which is much higher than in the general population.

Your privacy

There are no accounts, no tracking and no advertising.

Glossary

Blood glucose
The amount of sugar in your blood, measured in mg/dL or mmol/L.
Glucose tolerance test
A lab test that measures blood glucose two hours after you drink a sugary solution.
HbA1c
A blood test that shows your average blood sugar over the past two to three months.
BMI
Body mass index, a measure of weight relative to height. It is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres, squared.
AUC
A score from 0.5 to 1.0 showing how well a model separates people who have a condition from those who do not. 0.5 is no better than chance.
Cross-validation
A way of testing a model by repeatedly training it on part of the data and checking it on the rest.
